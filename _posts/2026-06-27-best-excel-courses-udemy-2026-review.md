## The Problem With Finding the Right Excel Course

You've decided to improve your Excel skills. That's practical and sensible — Excel competence genuinely improves your earning potential and job security. But Udemy has thousands of Excel courses. The ratings are sometimes misleading. Course descriptions are often vague. And you've only got a limited budget and even more limited free time.

This article cuts through that noise. I've reviewed the major Excel courses on Udemy that are actually worth your money in 2026. I'll tell you what each course teaches, who it's for, what it costs, and whether the time investment pays off.

## How I've Evaluated These Courses

Before I recommend anything, here's my criteria:

- **Instructor quality**: Can they explain clearly without padding the content?
- **Practical focus**: Does the course teach you skills you can use immediately?
- **Current content**: Are the videos up to date with modern Excel versions?
- **Honest pacing**: No 40-hour courses that could be 12 hours
- **Student feedback**: Real ratings from people who've completed the course
- **Value for money**: Price relative to what you actually learn

Udemy prices fluctuate constantly. Most courses cost £10–£15 during sales (which happen regularly). Full price is typically £40–£200, but you'll rarely pay that if you wait for a discount.

## Should You Take a Full "Excel Masterclass"?

Udemy advertises many "complete Excel courses" that promise to teach you everything from basics to advanced in 40+ hours. Honestly? Skip them.

Here's why: You don't need to sit through basics if you already know them. And if you're genuinely a beginner, a 40-hour course is demoralising. You don't see progress for weeks.

Better approach: Take 2–3 shorter, focused courses based on what you actually need.

- **Only know basic formulas?** Take the Advanced Excel Formulas course (9.5 hours), not a 40-hour masterclass.
- **Need to automate work?** Take the VBA Macros course (22 hours), skip the dashboard module that's irrelevant to you.
- **Building reports for your boss?** Take the Dashboards course (8 hours), not four hours of "introduction to Excel" padding.

## How to Actually Complete a Course (Not Just Buy It)

Most people buy online courses and never finish them. Here's how to be different:

**1. Set a specific completion deadline**

Don't say "I'll do this course." Say "I'll finish section 3 by Friday." Specific deadlines work. Vague intentions don't.

**2. Watch videos at 1.25x or 1.5x speed**

The instructor won't mind. You'll save 30% of your time and still absorb the content.

**3. Do the practice exercises immediately after each video**

Don't batch them. Don't skip them. If you watch passively, you'll forget everything in a week.

**4. Build your own project alongside the course**

Instead of just following the instructor's example, apply what you're learning to your actual work. You'll remember it better and get immediate value.

**5. Keep notes**

Write down formulas, keyboard shortcuts, and principles you want to remember. Your notes are worth more than rewatching the videos.
